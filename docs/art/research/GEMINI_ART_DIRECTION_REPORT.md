# Art Direction & Visual Mood Board Development

**Source:** Gemini report supplied by the user on 2026-11-15.  
**Status:** Research input; not every proposal is an approved production decision.

## Executive Summary

The report proposes a **Whimsical Gothic Storybook** aesthetic balancing 40% cozy nostalgia, 30% whimsical humor, and 30% mysterious gothic unease. Its goal is to translate domestic sabotage, stealthy observation and the atmospheric transformation of a family home into a cohesive visual identity.

## 1. Visual Research Report

### Overall Art Direction & Storybook Style
- References: *Over the Garden Wall*, *The Secret of Kells*, *Song of the Sea*, Arthur Rackham and Tove Jansson.
- Characteristics: organic ink contours, layered gouache textures, muted autumn palettes, cozy domesticity blended with whimsical mystery.
- Adaptable principles: painterly background washes, implied depth without hard perspective grids, environments framed like storybook pages.
- Avoid: flat corporate vector graphics, hyper-detailed digital painting without hand-drawn charm, overly bright saturated palettes.

### Interior Environments & Victorian Architecture
- References: late 19th-century English country houses, Arts and Crafts interiors (William Morris wallpapers), Edwardian parlors.
- Characteristics: walnut paneling, heavy damask curtains, patterned wool rugs, grand stone fireplaces.
- Adaptable principles: simplify architectural clutter into readable gameplay shapes while retaining worn wood, brass and woven fabric.
- Avoid: claustrophobic pitch-black dungeon aesthetics and sterile modern minimalism.

### Character Design & Silhouettes
- References: Edward Gorey, Ronald Searle and gothic-comedy illustration such as *The Addams Family*.
- Characteristics: distinct silhouettes, exaggerated proportions, expressive postures and subtle asymmetry.
- Adaptable principles: communicate routines and emotional states through posture and silhouette.
- Avoid: generic anime proportions and rigid symmetry.

### Environmental Props & Object Design
- References: antique brass clocks, Victorian tea sets, carved mahogany armchairs and gilded picture frames.
- Characteristics: hand-crafted imperfections, rounded edges, worn patinas and distinct material separation.
- Adaptable principles: clear silhouette contrast for interactive sabotage targets.
- Avoid: artificial-looking identical repeated modular assets.

### Lighting, Atmosphere & Color
- References: Rembrandt chiaroscuro, autumn twilight and candlelight illustration.
- Characteristics: warm amber/golden highlights contrasted with deep dusk-blue shadows; soft light falloff.
- Adaptable principles: lighting acts as an emotional barometer as the house shifts from warm golden light to cold haunted moonlight.
- Avoid: applying a uniform blue filter as the only Haunted treatment.

### Linework & Surface Textures
- References: traditional pen-and-ink cross-hatching, Japanese woodblock prints and watercolor on cold-press paper.
- Characteristics: muted dark-brown/charcoal lines with variable weight and subtle paper grain.
- Adaptable principles: organic contours on interactive elements; softer background transitions.
- Avoid: pure black outlines and pixel-perfect vector strokes.

### Game Art Implementation
- References: *Don't Starve: Hamlet* for top-down/isometric architectural framing and organic asset distribution; *Oxenfree* for atmospheric layering.
- Adaptable principles: separable layers and hierarchy of characters > interactive props > furniture > background atmosphere.

## 2. Primary Mood Board Specification

The report proposes a 16:9 board with nine zones:
1. World and atmosphere: Victorian manor, autumn ivy, amber twilight.
2. Interior environments: living room, library and staircase.
3. Warm vs. Haunted living room.
4. Ghost and six resident family members.
5. Interactive prop library.
6. Linework and texture studies.
7. Color swatches from Old Parchment to Ghost Cyan.
8. Candlelight, daylight and spectral moonbeam studies.
9. Gameplay visual language for environmental decay.

## 3. Supplementary Boards

- Board A: materials and surfaces — fabric, wood, paper grain and ink weights.
- Board B: lighting and atmospheric shifts — Cozy to Haunted.
- Board C: character and ghost interaction — cyan translucency against warm interior light and shadows.

## 5. Living Room Art Test Proposed by Gemini

Core elements: grand stone fireplace, two mismatched velvet armchairs, tufted sofa, overflowing bookshelf, tall bay window with heavy drapes and antique mantel clock.

Suggested character staging: father reading by the hearth; ghost hovering near the ceiling.

Evaluation criteria:
1. Style coherence.
2. Warm/Haunted state transformation without losing spatial clarity.
3. Immediate readability of interactive targets such as fireplace flue and clock pendulum.
4. Separation of background, props and character sprites.
